What Is a SIEM and How Does It Work?

Follow security events from collection and correlation to investigation and documented response.

What is a SIEM?

SIEM means Security Information and Event Management. It collects security-related logs from systems, normalises or structures information and creates searches or alerts when configured conditions are met. A SIEM also helps analysts investigate a sequence of events across time and systems.

How a SIEM works

Data collection and retention

Sources send logs through agents, syslog, APIs or other integrations. The SIEM may parse data into fields such as user, host, time and action. Retention controls determine how much history is available for searches or investigations.

Correlation and alerts

Correlation combines conditions or sequences: for example, repeated failed sign-ins followed by a successful sign-in from a new location. Rules are imperfect; tuning is needed to reduce false alerts without hiding meaningful activity.

What SIEM is not

A SIEM is not automatically a Security Operations Centre (SOC). A SOC is a function or team that includes processes, tools, analysts and incident response. SIEM is one component that may support monitoring.

Lab experience distinction: A personal Wazuh lab can demonstrate agents, detection rules and dashboards. It does not establish professional SOC coverage or claim that an organisation is continuously monitored.

Benefits and limitations

  • Benefits: central visibility, searchable history, repeatable detections and investigation context.
  • Limits: visibility only includes sources that provide logs; rules can miss or misclassify activity.
  • Operational burden: licensing, storage, parsing, tuning and alert response require ongoing ownership.

Best practices

  • Start with valuable, reliable log sources.
  • Synchronise system time and verify source health.
  • Document alert purpose and response steps.
  • Measure false positives and missed cases.
  • Protect SIEM accounts and administrative data.

Frequently asked questions

Does a SIEM detect every attack?

No. Detection depends on available telemetry, rule quality and attacker behaviour. A SIEM should complement prevention, endpoint and identity controls.

Can a small business use a SIEM?

It can, especially when a provider manages it, but collection, storage and response should match the business risk and available skills.

Key takeaways

A SIEM helps collect and correlate evidence, but alerts need context and action. Success depends on source quality, tuning, analyst process and incident response ownership.
