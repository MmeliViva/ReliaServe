How to Improve Business Email Security

Protect mail accounts and domains through stronger authentication, safer configuration and staff awareness.

Beginner 9 min read

Learning objectives

  • Identify common business email risks.
  • Explain MFA and conditional account controls.
  • Describe SPF, DKIM and DMARC at a conceptual level.
  • Apply safer invoice and payment verification practices.

What makes email a security risk?

Email is both a communication tool and an entry point to credentials, financial processes and sensitive information. Attackers may impersonate suppliers, send malicious attachments or create links that resemble trusted websites. A South African business should consider both account takeover and payment fraud.

Secure account access

Start by requiring multi-factor authentication for mail and administrative accounts. MFA means more than one independent factor verifies identity, so it is more difficult for a stolen password alone to grant access. Use a password manager, remove unused accounts and review sign-in alerts.

  • Restrict legacy or basic authentication if the mail platform supports modern authentication.
  • Review risky sign-in and mailbox forwarding rules.
  • Protect financial recipients through independent payment verification.
  • Limit administrator and bulk sender access.

SPF, DKIM and DMARC

SPF publishes systems authorised to send mail for a domain. DKIM adds a cryptographic signature that can confirm a message was not changed and was authorised by the signing domain. DMARC tells receiving systems how to handle SPF or DKIM alignment failures and provides reporting.

Implement domains in stages, beginning with monitoring and legitimate-source inventory, rather than immediately rejecting unknown mail without understanding the effect on suppliers and application mail.

Employee awareness

  • Verify unexpected payment or banking-detail changes using a known telephone number.
  • Check the full sender address and hover over links; do not rely on display names alone.
  • Report suspicious messages through the organisation's reporting process.
  • Do not enter credentials on a page reached unexpectedly by email.

Practical example: An account administrator receives an urgent “supplier account suspended” email. Instead of signing in through the link, the administrator opens the provider site using a known bookmark and checks for genuine notices.

Benefits and limitations

Email controls reduce account takeover and spoofing, but an authorised sender account can still send malicious content. Use email controls together with endpoint protection, payment verification and reporting.

Frequently asked questions

Should DMARC be set to reject immediately?

Not without first identifying legitimate senders and reviewing reports. Premature enforcement can block legitimate mail.

Can MFA stop every account attack?

No. MFA significantly improves security but users can approve unexpected prompts or become victims of other social engineering.
